# Copy that sounds like a product

A block is read, not admired. Its words are part of the design: the headline sets
the hierarchy, a label sets the tone, and a button is a promise. The judge scores
this as `copy`, and what it reads as "nobody wrote this" is a short list of shapes.

## What good copy does here

- **One voice.** The same person wrote the headline, the labels and the footnote.
- **Specific.** A real noun, a real number, a real place. Invented is fine; vague
  is not.
- **Sentence case** in headings and labels, unless the brand is shouting on purpose.
- **A button says what happens.** "Start the trial", not "Submit", never "Click
  here".
- **It promises only what the block can show.** A demo block has no signup, no
  checkout and no dashboard behind it. Describe what is on the page.

## The shapes that read as machine-written

Each one is a default, not a rule of grammar: a person can use it on purpose. Fix
the ones that arrived by habit.

1. **The contrast nobody asked for.** "It is not just a scheduler, it is a way to
   own your time." The negative half names something no one claimed, and the
   sentence sounds larger for it. Say the thing: "It schedules the week and keeps
   the calendar in one place." Keep a contrast only when the negative half corrects
   a belief the reader actually holds.
2. **The closer that repeats.** A one-line paragraph after the point, saying the
   point again: "That is the difference." "That is the real win." Cut it. The
   section ends on its last fact.
3. **The forced triad.** "Fast, secure and scalable." Three adjectives because
   three sound complete. Keep the one that is true and prove it: "Syncs in under a
   second."
4. **Inflated significance.** "Marks a pivotal moment", "a testament to", "shapes
   the future of". State what happened: "Founded in 1989."
5. **Sales fog.** Seamless, powerful, elevate, unlock, next level, cutting-edge,
   game-changing. Replace with the capability: "Works offline."
6. **Borrowed authority.** "Trusted by industry leaders", "experts agree". A name,
   or nothing.
7. **Placeholder content.** Acme Inc., Lorem ipsum, "Your text here", "Your
   tagline". Invent a product and commit to it.
8. **Decoration inside the sentence.** Title Case In Every Heading, emoji and
   arrows as bullets, a bold label and a colon in every list item. Sentence case,
   no decoration, and prose when the labels say nothing.
9. **The dash as default glue.** If two clauses need a relationship, choose it:
   period, comma, colon, parentheses.
10. **Chat residue.** "Here is how it works", "Let's dive in", "I hope this helps".
    The block is not a reply.
11. **The same closer after every section.** Three sections, three "That's why…".
    One block, one idea, no refrain.

## Working with a demo product

A brief rarely names the company, so invent one: a name, three prices, a place, a
year. Then keep them consistent everywhere. A price in the table that contradicts
the price in the paragraph is the same defect as a wrong number.

Testimonials are allowed when they are as specific as the rest: a name, a role, and
a sentence someone could actually say about this product. A quote that could sit
under any logo is filler with a photo next to it; leave the section out instead.

## The test

Read every string aloud. If it could sit on any product's page, it is not this
product's page. If it says something the block cannot show, it is a lie with good
typography.
